From an open shift to a covered one

A home that is short for Saturday night should not have to phone around. Here is how a shift gets filled.

  1. A home posts the shift

    Date, hours, pay per hour or per shift, and the credentials the caregiver needs.

  2. Caregivers who are free see it

    Each caregiver marks the days and nights they can work. Shifts that fit are labelled for them.

  3. They apply, or the home offers

    The home sees each person's credentials and rating, accepts someone, and both sides can message.

  4. Both sides rate the shift

    After the shift, the caregiver rates the home and the home rates the caregiver.

Professional care training, with the lessons online

Washington asks new long-term care workers for 75 hours of training. We are building a course that lets you study the lessons online, around your shifts, and come in for the hands-on skills practice.

  • 5 hours orientation and safety
  • 38 hours fundamentals of caregiving
  • 16 hours hands-on skills practice

Hours as set out by the Washington State Department of Social and Health Services. Our course is not open yet and is subject to state approval.

Why homes and caregivers can rely on it

Credentials are checked by a person

A "Verified" label means someone at ShiftKind looked at the document. Homes still run the state background check themselves.

Ratings are earned

Only a home and a caregiver who shared a shift can rate each other, once, after it happens.

Your details stay yours

Homes see a caregiver's first name and surname initial. Phone numbers and email are shared only when you choose to share them.

Caregivers never pay

Create a profile, add your credentials and pick up shifts at no cost.

Homes start with 30 days free

Post shifts and search for caregivers during the pilot. After that, a monthly package keeps your home active.
